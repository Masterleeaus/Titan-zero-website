Practical guide

Measure missed service enquiries before adding tools

For owners who suspect they miss enquiries but do not know how many. Count them for a few weeks first, then decide what to change.

Create one reliable count

For a short period, record each enquiry, channel, time received, first response, result and reason it did not proceed. Decide how you will treat duplicates and existing customers before counting.

This creates a baseline that is more useful than a best-case forecast or a remembered busy week.

Separate missed contact from lost revenue

Not every unanswered call would have become a job. Some enquiries are outside your service area, some are price checks and some would not have been accepted even if answered.

Use your own conversion rate and average job value in the calculators. Read the result as opportunity exposure, not a promised recovery or software return.

Make the response process explicit

Name the response owner, expected callback window and next step for an unanswered call or web enquiry. Keep a fallback for periods when the normal owner is on a job.

Only after the process is clear should you compare phone handling, inbox, booking or automation options. Confirm what each service can do and what human review remains necessary.

Review by channel and job type

Compare outcomes across phone, email, web forms and referrals. A single overall conversion rate can hide a strong channel or a service type that needs a different response.

Repeat the review after a process change. If volume, response time or conversion did not improve, update the assumption rather than claiming a result that the data does not show.

Set a response promise only when you can keep it

This guide does not publish a response promise. A fixed reply time should appear on your website only after you have measured your response times for a set period and met that time in practice.

Until then, describe your intention in general terms. Do not describe a result you have not measured.

How the business tracks response time

For each enquiry, record when it arrived (phone, email, web form or message), when the first reply was sent and who sent it. Work out the share of first replies sent within one business hour each week, and review the misses.

Keep this log by hand or in your own spreadsheet until you have confirmed what your enquiry tools record.

What counts as a first reply

A first reply is a message from a person. An automated acknowledgement counts only if it names your business and says who will follow up and when. A message that only confirms receipt does not count as a human response.

Keep promotions out of enquiry replies

A reply to an enquiry should answer that enquiry. Do not add an offer, newsletter or other promotion to it unless the customer has separately agreed to receive offers. Record that consent with the time, the form and the wording shown.

Pending legal review: whether a plain reply to an enquiry is a commercial message under the Spam Act 2003 has not been confirmed by a lawyer. Get that advice before relying on this rule for any reply that also promotes something.

This guide is general information for planning a business workflow. It is not tax, legal, safety or employment advice.
