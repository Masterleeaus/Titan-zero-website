Practical guide

Make invoice follow-up a clear workflow

For anyone with invoices sitting unpaid. Use this sequence so every invoice has an amount, a due date and a named person who follows it up.

Prepare the invoice from the completed work

Confirm the customer, service address, approved scope, completion date, agreed price and any documented variation. Attach the evidence or purchase details the customer expects to receive.

If a job is still waiting on a sign-off or supporting information, record the missing item and who owns it instead of letting the invoice disappear into a general queue.

Show the due date and next action

Use clear payment terms and make the due date easy to find. Choose a reminder cadence that fits your agreements and the customer relationship.

Keep a record of reminders, promises to pay, disputes and payment plans. Escalate exceptions to a person who can review the context.

Track cash timing separately from sales

The invoice calculator estimates the value beyond terms and the hours spent chasing payment. It does not count an overdue amount as lost revenue or treat faster collection as a new sale.

Use a cash-flow forecast to model when receipts may arrive and when outgoing payments are due. For tax reporting and debt recovery decisions, get advice that fits the business and customer agreement.

Review the cause, not only the balance

Group delays by missing purchase order, disputed scope, incorrect details, customer process or late payment. Fix the repeated cause where possible.

Recheck the process monthly and compare overdue value, time spent and the age of unresolved exceptions.

Quote follow-up

Quotes that are not answered need their own short sequence. See the two-step quote follow-up guide, which sets the cadence, stop rules and consent checks.

This guide is general information for planning a business workflow. It is not tax, legal, safety or employment advice.
