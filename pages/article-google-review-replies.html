Practical guide

Reply to every Google review, with approved templates

Three ready-to-edit templates for positive, negative and star-only reviews, plus the rules that keep every reply safe.

Reply to every review, good and bad

Reply to each Google review, whether it is positive, negative or star-only. A reply shows future customers that the business listens. Replies are public, so keep them short, calm and free of personal detail.

Use the templates below as a starting point. Change the wording to suit your business, but keep the rules in the next section.

Template 1: thank-you (positive review)

Thank you for taking the time to leave a review. We are glad the work met your expectations, and we appreciate you choosing [business name].

Use this for four and five star reviews, and for any review that praises the work without raising a problem.

Template 2: complaint (negative review)

Thank you for telling us about your experience. We are sorry the job did not meet your expectations. We take feedback like this seriously. Please contact us on [phone or email] so we can look into it directly.

Do not explain the job, dispute the facts, name the customer or mention price or payment in the public reply. Move the conversation to a private channel.

Template 3: no written review (star rating only)

Thank you for your rating and for choosing [business name]. We appreciate your business.

Use this when a review has a star rating but no text, so the reply still acknowledges the customer.

Rules for every reply

Do not include the customer's name, address, phone number, job details or photos in a reply. Do not argue about the job or about the customer's account in public.

Never offer a discount, free work or any other reward in exchange for a review or for a changed review. Google does not allow this, and it can mislead customers.

Reviews are never removed or hidden by the business

Your business never deletes, hides or suppresses a review, including negative reviews. The ACCC guidance on online reviews says businesses must not hide negative reviews.

If you believe a review breaks Google's policies, report it through your Google Business Profile. Google decides whether to remove it. Do not delete or hide it yourself, and do not ask customers to remove reviews.

Human approval before any reply is posted

Every reply is written, checked and posted by a person at the business. If a plugin or other tool drafts replies, nothing is posted until a person has read and approved that reply.

Keep a record of replies you post, so you can see how each complaint was handled.

Timing

Reply to new reviews as soon as you can. Set a weekly time to check your profile so no review is left unanswered for long. Be cautious about promising a specific response time in public.

This guide is general information for planning a business workflow. It is not tax, legal, safety or employment advice.
