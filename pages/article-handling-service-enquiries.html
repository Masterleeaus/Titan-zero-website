Practical guide

Measure missed service enquiries before adding tools

Count enquiries, response times and outcomes before deciding whether to change the process or add a channel.

Create one reliable count

For a short period, record each enquiry, channel, time received, first response, result and reason it did not proceed. Decide how you will treat duplicates and existing customers before counting.

This creates a baseline that is more useful than a best-case forecast or a remembered busy week.

Separate missed contact from lost revenue

Not every unanswered call would have become a job. Some enquiries are outside your service area, some are price checks and some would not have been accepted even if answered.

Use your own conversion rate and average job value in the calculators. Read the result as opportunity exposure, not a promised recovery or software return.

Make the response process explicit

Name the response owner, expected callback window and next step for an unanswered call or web enquiry. Keep a fallback for periods when the normal owner is on a job.

Only after the process is clear should you compare phone handling, inbox, booking or automation options. Confirm what each service can do and what human review remains necessary.

Review by channel and job type

Compare outcomes across phone, email, web forms and referrals. A single overall conversion rate can hide a strong channel or a service type that needs a different response.

Repeat the review after a process change. If volume, response time or conversion did not improve, update the assumption rather than claiming a result that the data does not show.
