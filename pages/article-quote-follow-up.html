Practical guide

Follow up a quote twice, then stop

A two-step cadence, clear stop rules and the consent checks that apply before any follow-up is sent.

Two follow-ups, then stop

Send one follow-up two to three days after the quote. Send a second follow-up about three days after the first. After that, stop. Two follow-ups are the most a quote gets from this sequence.

Industry practice is not the same as a tested result. Track how many quotes are accepted after each step, and change the cadence only if your own numbers show a reason.

Choose the channel

Email is the main channel. Use text only if the customer has said they prefer it. Use a phone call only when email and text have not worked, and only within the calling times in the next section.

When to stop

Stop the sequence as soon as any of these happens:

  • The customer accepts the quote or books the job.
  • The customer declines the quote.
  • The customer asks you to stop, by any means.
  • The customer says "not now". Pause the sequence. Only contact them again if they ask you to, or on a date they chose.
  • The second follow-up has been sent.

What every message must include

Each follow-up names your business. It includes an unsubscribe option that is free to use and that is honoured within five working days. The unsubscribe must keep working for at least 30 days.

Keep the message about the quote. Do not add an offer or promotion to a quote follow-up.

Who can be contacted

Do not send a quote follow-up to anyone who has not consented to receive marketing messages. The sequence is blocked for those contacts, and the block should be checked before every send.

Pending legal review: whether a quote reply or quote follow-up is a commercial message under the Spam Act 2003 has not been confirmed by a lawyer. The plugin sequence stays switched off until that review is complete.

Calling times for phone follow-ups

Phone follow-ups, where they are allowed, happen Monday to Friday 9am to 8pm and Saturday 9am to 5pm. Do not call on Sunday or on public holidays. Say who you are and your business name, and show a return number.

This guide is general information for planning a business workflow. It is not tax, legal, safety or employment advice.
