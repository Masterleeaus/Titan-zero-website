Practical guide

Plan service capacity around productive hours

Use bookable time and real job duration to see where scheduling pressure begins.

Separate paid hours from bookable hours

Paid hours include travel, preparation, stock runs, customer updates, breaks, leave and admin. Only part of that time is available for scheduled work.

Estimate productive hours from actual rosters and completed jobs over a representative period. If demand changes by season, compare busy and quiet periods rather than relying on one average.

Use the whole job duration

Include setup, pack-down, access, travel between customers and the time needed to close out the record. For teams, use the crew hours consumed by the job, not only the time the customer sees.

Group work by location or skill where possible. A team can have unused hours and still lack the right person, vehicle or equipment for a particular booking.

Compare capacity with booked work

The capacity planner gives a simple monthly estimate. Compare it with existing jobs, recurring visits, leave, cancellation patterns and work that must be kept for urgent requests.

A gap in the arithmetic is not automatically spare capacity. Check travel, skills, equipment and location before offering more bookings.

Turn the estimate into a weekly review

Track planned hours, completed hours, travel and reschedules for a few weeks. Update the assumptions when the actual pattern differs from the estimate.

If hiring is under consideration, model the role’s full cost and confirm the applicable pay and employment settings before deciding.

This guide is general information for planning a business workflow. It is not tax, legal, safety or employment advice.
