# Service-business marketing: 10 tactics for Titan Zero

**Date of research:** 18 November 2026
**Scope:** Australian trades, cleaning, handyman, gardening, pool, beauty and mobile service businesses, and how software is sold to them.
**Status:** Research note only. No site or product code was changed.

## How to read this

Each tactic gives: why it works, the evidence and where it came from, what to build or change on the site, and the legal limit to respect. Evidence is graded in plain words:

- **Primary** means a regulator, Google or a government body.
- **Research** means a named study, though it may be old or overseas.
- **Vendor** means a software or marketing company selling something. Treat its numbers as claims.

Sources that are old or from overseas are marked as such.

## Quick summary

1. Keep the Google Business Profile complete for every suburb or area served.
2. Ask for Google reviews with Google's own review link, only after a real job.
3. Reply to every review, good and bad.
4. Answer every enquiry within the hour.
5. Send a text back to a missed call, with no promotional content in it.
6. Follow up a quote twice, then stop.
7. Capture marketing consent on the form, separate from job messages.
8. Publish practical trade answers written from real jobs.
9. Follow WordPress.org directory rules when listing the plugin.
10. Post project photos on the profile, with the customer's consent.

Referral programs are left out of the top 10. I did not find a primary source that tested them for Australian trades, so they need separate research.

## 1. Keep the Google Business Profile complete for every area served

**Why it works.** Google says local results mainly depend on relevance (how well the profile matches the search), distance (from the searcher), and prominence (how well known the business is, including links and review count and rating). Owners can improve results by verifying the business, keeping details accurate, replying to reviews and adding photos. Google also says there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking. [Primary: Google, Business Profile ranking help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en-GB)

Google's 2026 services playbook adds specifics for service-area businesses. It allows up to 20 service areas, one primary category and up to nine secondary categories, and says the boundaries should stay within about two hours' drive from the base. [Primary: Google services Business Profile playbook 2026](https://services.google.com/fh/files/helpcenter/services_gbp_best_practices_playbook_2026.pdf)

**Build or change on the site.** Add a short "Google profile setup" checklist to the resource pages. Each trade page in `industries/` should link to the suburb areas and categories a business should set. Do not invent suburbs or addresses on the site.

**Legal limit.** The profile must show accurate information. Do not list areas or categories the business does not serve. Ranking claims on the site must not promise a position.

## 2. Ask for Google reviews with Google's own link or QR code, after a real job

**Why it works.** Most local customers read reviews. In BrightLocal's 2024 survey of 1,141 US consumers, 75% said they always or regularly read reviews, and half said they trust online reviews about as much as advice from friends and family. [Research (US, 2024): BrightLocal Local Consumer Review Survey 2024](https://brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey-2024/)

Google now lets a business create a review link or QR code from its profile. The link can be shared by email, WhatsApp or text, or shown on a receipt or in the store. [Primary: Google, review link and QR code](https://support.google.com/business/answer/16816815?hl=en)

**Build or change on the site.** Add a "how to ask for reviews" guide to `resources/`. It should cover timing (send after the job is signed off), wording, and where to place the link. A plugin feature could send the link at job completion, with an opt-out.

**Legal limit.** Google prohibits giving free or discounted goods or services in exchange for reviews. Reviews must reflect a real experience. [Primary: Google, review link and QR code](https://support.google.com/business/answer/16816815?hl=en) The ACCC's guidance says businesses must not write or commission fake reviews, must be open about paid or incentivised reviews, and must not hide negative reviews. Note that this ACCC guidance is from 2013 and may be partly out of date. [Primary, dated: ACCC media release on online reviews](https://accc.gov.au/media-release/accc-releases-guidance-about-online-product-reviews) Google can also restrict a profile for fake engagement. [Primary: Google, profile restrictions](https://support.google.com/business/answer/14114287)

## 3. Reply to every review, good and bad

**Why it works.** Google's playbook says 91% of consumers use reviews to judge local businesses, and that 65% are more likely to choose a business that responds to them. Replies to both positive and negative reviews are recommended. [Primary (Google's own playbook; the figures are as stated by Google): services GBP playbook 2026](https://services.google.com/fh/files/helpcenter/services_gbp_best_practices_playbook_2026.pdf)

**Build or change on the site.** Provide reply templates for thank-you, complaint and no-reply cases in the resource pages. A later plugin feature could draft replies for the owner to approve. A human must approve every reply before posting.

**Legal limit.** Do not reply to a review with personal details of the customer, and do not argue about the job in public. Do not remove or hide a negative review through the business's own action, since the ACCC treats suppression as misleading. [ACCC guidance](https://accc.gov.au/media-release/accc-releases-guidance-about-online-product-reviews)

## 4. Answer every enquiry within the hour

**Why it works.** A Harvard study of 1.25 million sales leads found that firms contacting a lead within an hour were nearly seven times as likely to qualify it as firms that waited an hour longer, and more than 60 times as likely as firms that waited a day or more. This is an older US study (published around 2011), as reported by an Australian business site. [Research (older, US): Harvard study as reported by SmartCompany](https://www.smartcompany.com.au/marketing/20110704-are-you-letting-online-leads-go-cold/) The same report says 23% of companies in the audit never replied at all.

Trade software vendors market the same idea. Tradify's enquiry page says enquiries from forms, email and calls should be captured in one place and answered quickly from a phone. It makes no measured claim about missed enquiries. [Vendor: Tradify, customer enquiry software](https://www.tradifyhq.com/au/features/customer-enquiry-software)

**Build or change on the site.** The existing `resources/handling-service-enquiries/` page should state a clear response promise (for example, "we reply within one business hour") and explain how the business keeps that promise. Do not state the promise as a guarantee until the product actually meets it.

**Legal limit.** A plain reply to a person who enquired is generally a service message rather than marketing. I did not confirm this from a primary source, so treat it as a working assumption. Any promotional content added to the reply makes it a commercial message. Such content must follow the Spam Act rules in tactic 6 and tactic 7.

## 5. Text back after a missed call

**Why it works.** The evidence here is weak. Most sources are vendors selling missed-call text tools. One vendor article claims up to 85% of callers will not call back after one miss, and that text-back tools recover 30% to 60% of leads, but it names no source for these figures. [Vendor, unverified figures: Signpost, missed calls for contractors](https://www.signpost.com/blog/how-much-business-do-contractors-lose-from-missed-calls/) Treat these numbers as a hypothesis to test, not a fact to publish.

**Build or change on the site.** Build the text-back feature in the plugin, not as a claim on the site. On the site, describe it in plain words and avoid any percentage. Track the result in a pilot: count missed calls, texts sent and jobs booked, for 30 days.

**Legal limit.** The text must identify the business by name and include a way to opt out if it is marketing. A text that only says "sorry we missed you, what can we do" is an ordinary reply, but adding a promotion makes it a commercial message. Check any text template against tactic 7 first. Register alphanumeric sender IDs on the SMS Sender ID Register where used. [Primary: business.gov.au, promoting by email or text](https://business.gov.au/marketing-and-advertising/promoting-your-business-by-email-or-text-messages)

## 6. Follow up a quote twice, then stop

**Why it works.** Trade software vendors advise a first follow-up two to three days after the quote, then one more a few days later, then to move on. They also suggest using email as the main channel, text for clients who prefer it, and the phone only when other channels fail. [Vendor: Jobber, how to follow up on a quote](https://getjobber.com/academy/how-to-follow-up-on-quote/) This is industry practice, not a tested study. Test it on your own quotes.

**Build or change on the site.** Publish the cadence on the resource page for quotes and in the invoice follow-up guide (`resources/invoice-follow-up/`). In the plugin, add a quote follow-up sequence with two steps, stop rules and a "not now" option.

**Legal limit.** A quote follow-up is a commercial message in many cases. The ACMA says consent is needed before sending commercial messages. Express consent is best; inferred consent applies only where the customer gave an address and would reasonably expect marketing from the business. The message must identify the business and include a working unsubscribe that is free and honoured within five working days. The unsubscribe must keep working for at least 30 days. [Primary: ACMA, avoid sending spam](https://www.acma.gov.au/avoid-sending-spam) [Primary: business.gov.au, promoting by email or text](https://business.gov.au/marketing-and-advertising/promoting-your-business-by-email-or-text-messages) I did not confirm from a primary source whether a reply to a quote request falls outside the commercial-message rules. Get advice before relying on that.

## 7. Capture marketing consent on the enquiry form, separately from job messages

**Why it works.** A clean consent record lets a business email or text customers without risk. It also separates service messages (about the job) from marketing messages (offers and newsletters), which keeps unsubscribe rates lower.

**Build or change on the site.** Add two unticked boxes to any enquiry or booking form on the site and in the plugin: one for job messages, one for offers. Store the time, the form and the wording used as the consent record.

**Legal limit.** Under the Spam Act, the business must have consent before sending a marketing message, must keep records of express consent, and must give a working unsubscribe. [Primary: ACMA, avoid sending spam](https://www.acma.gov.au/avoid-sending-spam) The Privacy Act's direct marketing rules (APP 7) add that a business must offer a simple opt-out and must honour stop requests, usually within 30 days. If a business uses information from a third party, or where the person would not expect marketing, the rules are stricter. [Primary: OAIC, APP 7 guidelines](https://www.oaic.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0011/1235/app-guidelines-chapter-7-v1.1.pdf)

For phone marketing, the ACMA says businesses may not call numbers on the Do Not Call Register for telemarketing unless they have permission or are exempt. Telemarketing calls are allowed Monday to Friday 9am to 8pm and Saturday 9am to 5pm. No telemarketing on Sunday or public holidays. Callers must identify themselves and their business and show a return number. The ACMA page does not describe an existing-customer exemption. [Primary: ACMA, Do Not Call Register rules for telemarketers](https://www.acma.gov.au/protect-yourself-unwanted-calls-and-faxes)

## 8. Publish practical trade answers written from real jobs

**Why it works.** Google's guidance on helpful content asks whether a page shows first-hand experience, cites its sources and was written for people rather than search engines. It warns against pages made to catch search traffic, or padded or auto-generated pages. [Primary: Google Search Central, creating helpful content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)

**Build or change on the site.** Write one answer per common trade question, such as "how much does a pool clean cost" or "what should a quote include". Use the business's own job notes and photos, and name who wrote it. Add the existing `resources/` pages as examples. Do not publish price figures unless they match the approved catalogue. The site README already says prices must come from the approved catalogue.

**Legal limit.** Price and result claims must be accurate and must not mislead under the Australian Consumer Law. Do not present a sample job as a real customer story without permission.

## 9. List the WordPress plugin under the directory rules

**Why it works.** The plugin listing is the main free channel for a WordPress product. A listing that breaks the directory rules can be pulled, so compliance is itself a marketing tactic.

**What the rules say.** The WordPress.org guidelines cover several points relevant to marketing: plugins must respect trademarks and project names; plugins may not track users without consent; readme text must not be spammy; plugins may not hijack the admin screen; and trialware is not allowed. [Primary: WordPress.org plugin guidelines](https://github.com/WordPress/wporg-plugin-guidelines) The guideline text was only partly visible when I checked, so read the full guidelines before shipping.

**Build or change on the site.** Keep the plugin page on the site and the readme consistent. Do not add a paid upgrade banner inside the admin screen. Link to hosted services only with the user's consent.

**Legal limit.** Keep to the plugin rules and to the Spam Act for any message sent through the plugin. Do not use the names of other trade software brands in the listing unless the rules allow it.

## 10. Post project photos on the Google profile, with consent

**Why it works.** Google's playbook reports that profiles with photos get more direction requests and more website clicks. Its figures are from Google's own material, so treat them as directional. [Primary (figures as stated by Google): services GBP playbook 2026](https://services.google.com/fh/files/helpcenter/services_gbp_best_practices_playbook_2026.pdf)

**Build or change on the site.** Add a short guide to the resource pages on photos and posts: photograph the finished work, leave out the customer's face and house number unless they agree, and post monthly.

**Legal limit.** A photo that shows a customer, a home or an address may be personal information. Get written consent before posting one, and remove it when asked. Use the APP 7 and Privacy Act rules as the baseline. [Primary: OAIC, APP 7 guidelines](https://www.oaic.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0011/1235/app-guidelines-chapter-7-v1.1.pdf)

## Decisions made in this note (for review)

- Ranked tactics 1 to 10 by likely return and by how well the evidence supports them.
- Left referral programs out of the top 10, because no primary source was found.
- Treated all vendor and dated figures as claims, not facts.
- Did not confirm from a primary source whether a reply to a quote request counts as a commercial message. This is flagged for legal advice.

## Gaps

- No Australian industry association source was fetched (for example, trade or master builder bodies). This should be added.
- The OAIC direct marketing page could not be fetched. The APP 7 chapter was used instead.
- The ACCC review guidance dates from 2013.
- The Spam Act's quote-request rule was not confirmed from a primary source.
